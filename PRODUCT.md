# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Brands, bands, and businesses evaluating Ladies Taylor as a creative agency partner (Social Media Management, Branding, Packaging, Website Development), plus prospective students for their courses. They land here mid-decision, comparing agencies, and need to walk away convinced Ladies Taylor knows what they're doing and gets results — not just that the site looks nice.

## Product Purpose

A marketing and portfolio site for Ladies Taylor that showcases client work (bands, press/media features, events) and drives inquiries and course sign-ups. It's growing beyond a static portfolio into something more functional over time: a "brands of the month" feature, a blog, and courses — so it needs to hold up as a real, evolving content site, not a one-off launch page.

## Brand Personality

Ruthlessly honest, no fluff. The voice backs up its confidence with years of experience: it doesn't hedge, doesn't soften the pitch, and isn't afraid to be blunt or a little combative in service of getting to the point. Irreverent and playful on the surface (censored nav labels like "Cool Sh*t", "Pr*ss & M*edia") but that playfulness sits on top of real expertise, not in place of it.

## Anti-references

Not a generic, corporate-polished agency template — no soft gradients, no safe stock-photo positivity, no hedging copy. If it could be mistaken for a template-store "creative agency" site, it's wrong.

## Design Principles

- Confidence over decoration: bold color, hard-shadow "comic border" treatment, and blunt copy carry the brand more than ornamental polish does.
- Consistency reads as competence: a scattered type scale or drifting button styles undercut the "we know what we're doing" pitch just as much as weak copy would.
- Built to grow: new sections (brands of the month, blog, courses) should slot into the existing token system, not require one-off styling each time.
- Honest, not sanitized: keep the edge in the copy and tone; accessibility and consistency fixes should never flatten the personality into something generic.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Standard WCAG AA. No additional known accommodations beyond that (contrast, keyboard operability, labeled form fields, proper heading hierarchy).
